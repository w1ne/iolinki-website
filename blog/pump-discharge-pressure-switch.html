Pump discharge pressure: a switch point, a reset point and nothing that chatters

The PLC needs one clean signal: the pump has built pressure. With a set point and a reset point the signal does not chatter when the pressure ripples around the limit.

11 October 2026 · Andrii Shylenko

The job

A pump feeds a liquid line. The control has to know that the pump really delivers, not only that the motor contactor is closed. A dry-running pump, a closed valve upstream or a broken coupling all show up as missing discharge pressure. A pressure sensor on the discharge side gives that signal and the pressure value in one device.

In this example the pump runs at a little under 50 bar in normal operation. That is our plant's number; take yours from the pump curve or a gauge. We want “pressure OK” at 40 bar and above, and we want it to drop when the pressure falls to 37 bar.

The sensor

We used the ifm PN7092. The datasheet: measuring range 0 to 100 bar relative, liquids, medium temperature −25 to 80 °C, pressure rating 300 bar, minimum bursting pressure 650 bar, G 1/4 internal thread, response time under 3 ms. In the factory operating mode the set point SP is 1 to 100 bar, the reset point rP 0.5 to 99.5 bar, in steps of 0.5 bar, and SP and rP must be at least 0.5 bar apart.

Why two points? With one threshold, a pump that pulses around 40 bar makes the output flicker and the PLC logs a fault for every flicker. In hysteresis mode the output switches on at SP and only switches off at rP. The 3 bar gap is our choice. It has to be larger than the pressure ripple you see at the sensor.

The setting, checked

SP1 40 bar and rP1 37 bar are inside the datasheet ranges, on the 0.5 bar grid, and 3 bar apart. The studio checks the ranges and that rP sits below SP. It does not yet check the 0.5 bar minimum gap, so we checked that one against the datasheet by hand. Type 120 bar and the studio refuses: “Set point SP1 must be 1–100 bar on PN7092. 120 bar is outside.”

Output function normally open: the pin is 24 V while the pressure is OK. If your safety logic wants a wire break to look like a fault, choose normally closed and invert the logic in the PLC.

Master, port and cable

Port X1 of a Siemens SIMATIC ET 200eco PN 4 IO-Link (6ES7148-6JA00-0AB0). The PN7092 needs a class A port, and the Siemens datasheet gives a maximum unshielded cable length of 20 m for IO-Link. The master sits on the pump skid. The route in the model is 5.8 m; with 20 % slack the order has a 10 m M12 cable. The sensor draws less than 35 mA.

Run mode

In Run mode the pressure rises, holds, falls and rises again. The output switches on when the value crosses 40 bar and stays on until it drops to 37 bar. You can also drag the value yourself and stop between the two points to see that the output keeps its last state.

The process data is 16 bits. Pressure sits in bits 2 to 15 at 0.1 bar per count, the two switching outputs in bits 1 and 0. In the screenshot the master reads 07 A5: 0x07A5 shifted right by two is 489, that is 48.9 bar, and bit 0 is set because OUT1 is on.

The raw values come from the IODD scaling of 0.1 bar per count. Validation is vendor 310 and device 401, the device ID of the factory operating mode. The recommended cycle time is 2.3 ms, the device minimum. Write the three values with the master's ISDU function block or import the file, then read them back.

We did not set the switch-on and switch-off delays. The datasheet allows 0 to 50 s for dS and dr. If the PLC sees “pressure OK” missing for a moment during every pump start, mask the start in the PLC or add a delay on the sensor. The studio does not set the delays yet.
