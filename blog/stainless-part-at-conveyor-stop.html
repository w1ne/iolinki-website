A stainless part at a conveyor stop, detected with an IO-Link inductive sensor

The station only clamps or picks when the part is really sitting on the stop. Here the parts are stainless, and that changes the setting.

11 October 2026 · Andrii Shylenko

The job

A belt conveyor brings parts to a mechanical stop. A clamp, a robot or a press acts only when a part sits against the stop. One inductive sensor next to the stop tells the PLC that the part has arrived. This is one of the most common IO-Link applications in assembly lines.

Our parts are stainless steel, and the stop leaves a small gap between the part face and the sensor face. We want the output to switch when the part is within 3 mm of the sensor.

The sensor

We used the ifm IG6214, an M18 inductive sensor with IO-Link. The datasheet gives an IO-Link switch point of 1.4 to 7 mm, a measuring range of 0.75 to 7.5 mm over IO-Link, a hysteresis of 3 to 15 % of Sr and port class A. Over IO-Link it reports the distance as a process value, not only a switching bit.

The number that matters here is the correction factor. The datasheet lists steel 1, stainless steel 0.7, brass 0.5, aluminium 0.5 and copper 0.4. The switch point is set in steel terms. A stainless part has to come closer before the sensor sees the same signal.

The setting, checked

In the studio we picked stainless as the target material and typed 3 mm, the real distance we want at the machine. The studio divides by 0.7 and writes 4.29 mm steel-equivalent to the sensor. That is inside 1.4 to 7 mm, so the station opens with no notes.

If you ask for something the sensor cannot do, it says so. With 6 mm on stainless the studio answers: “Switching point 6 mm on stainless needs 8.57 mm steel-equivalent. IG6214 allows 1.4–7 mm, so stainless is reachable at 0.98–4.9 mm.” You find that out on the screen, not at the machine.

Output function normally open, PNP. Both are factory defaults in the IODD and both go into the commissioning file anyway, so a replaced sensor gets the same state.

Master, port and cable

The sensor goes to port X1 of an ifm AL1100, a 4-port PROFINET master with class A ports. It is mounted on the machine frame next to the stop, so only the fieldbus cable runs back to the cabinet. The AL1100 datasheet gives 3.6 A total sensor supply. The IG6214 draws less than 15 mA.

The cable route in the 3D model is 5.7 m. The studio adds 20 % slack and rounds up to a stock length, so the order has one 10 m M12 A-coded cable. IO-Link allows 20 m per port, and the report checks that too.

Run mode

Press Run and the conveyor moves. Parts slide onto the stop, wait there and leave. The IG6214 switches each time a part is held. The panel shows the output pin, 24 V while the part is there, and the 32-bit process data the master receives. Bit 0 is SSC1, the switching signal for our switch point, and the upper 16 bits carry the distance value.

Commissioning file and report

Export gives a commissioning file in JSON or CSV. Each port gets the device identity for validation (vendor 310, device 1344), the recommended cycle time (3.2 ms, the device minimum), data storage set to backup and restore, and the parameter writes as ISDU index, subindex and raw value:

One thing to know: the IODD gives the switch point as a raw range of 400 to 3800 with no unit. The studio maps that range linearly onto the datasheet's 1.4 to 7 mm. The same line maps the process value range onto 0.74 to 7.49 mm, which matches the datasheet measuring range of 0.75 to 7.5 mm. Read the value back once at commissioning.

The station report has the same data on paper: 3D picture, wiring, cables, ports and process data layout, power budget, parameter writes, bill of materials and an installer checklist. Print it or save it as PDF.

Why IO-Link here

If the line also runs brass or aluminium parts, the PLC can write a new switch point to index 60.1 on a product change. With data storage on, a replacement sensor gets the setting from the master. And the distance value tells you when a stop wears or a part sits crooked, before the switching signal starts to flicker.
