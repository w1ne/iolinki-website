A stainless part at a conveyor stop

The clamp only closes when the part really sits on the stop. With stainless parts the sensor has to be set differently.

11 October 2026 · Andrii Shylenko

The problem

Parts ride a belt to a mechanical stop, and a clamp or robot acts when one arrives. The parts are stainless. An inductive sensor set for steel sees them too late or not at all, so the clamp either waits forever or closes on nothing.

The station

  • Sensor ifm IG6214, M18 inductive, IO-Link, port class A
  • Master ifm AL1100, 4 ports, PROFINET, mounted on the machine
  • Port X1, M12 A-coded cable: 6.1 m route, 10 m ordered

The settings that matter

SettingValueWhyDatasheet / IODD
Target materialstainlessThe switch point is set in steel terms.Correction: steel 1, stainless 0.7, brass 0.5, aluminium 0.5, copper 0.4
Switching point3 mm at the part, 4.29 mm written3 mm / 0.7 = 4.29 mm steel-equivalentIO-Link switch point 1.4 to 7 mm, so stainless reaches 0.98 to 4.9 mm
Outputnormally open, PNP24 V while a part is heldNO/NC and PNP/NPN parameterisable

Type 6 mm on stainless and the studio stops you: it needs 8.57 mm steel-equivalent, and the sensor only goes to 7. The IODD gives the switch point as raw 400 to 3800 without a unit; the studio maps it linearly onto the datasheet's 1.4 to 7 mm, so read the value back once at commissioning.
