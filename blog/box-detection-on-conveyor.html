Box detection on a conveyor with a laser distance sensor

A distance sensor across the belt sees the boxes and ignores the guard rail behind them. Over IO-Link it also tells you how far away each box was.

11 October 2026 · Andrii Shylenko

The job

Cartons travel on a belt conveyor. The PLC counts them and starts the next station when one arrives. The sensor sits at the side of the belt and looks across it. On the far side there is a guard rail and sometimes a person walking by. Neither may count as a box.

The sensor

We used the ifm O5D100, a laser distance sensor with background suppression. The datasheet: measuring range 0.03 to 2 m on white paper with 90 % remission, background suppression from more than 2 m up to 20 m, red laser light at 650 nm, laser class 1, light spot under 5 mm at 2 m, switching frequency 15 Hz, extraneous light on the object at most 8 klx. The IODD sets the switch point SP1 from 5 to 200 cm.

The 90 % white figure matters. Brown cardboard reflects less, so check the switch point with your real boxes. The 15 Hz switching frequency matters too: the gap between two boxes, divided by the belt speed, must be longer than one switching cycle, or two boxes count as one.

The setting, checked

The belt is narrower than the distance to the guard rail. We set the switching distance to 500 mm: everything closer is a box, the rail behind it is ignored. 500 mm is inside the 50 to 2000 mm the IODD allows. Output normally open, so the pin is 24 V while a box is in front of the sensor.

The setting is in millimetres in the studio and in centimetres in the IODD. The commissioning file writes the raw value in the IODD unit: 500 mm becomes 50.

Master, port and cable

Port X1 of a Turck TBEN-S2-4IOL, a compact 4-port master that speaks PROFINET, EtherNet/IP and Modbus TCP. The Turck datasheet gives max. 4 A for the supply of ports C0 to C3 together and notes that this supply is not short-circuit proof, so protect the V2 supply in the cabinet. The O5D100 draws under 75 mA. The route is 4.7 m; the order has a 10 m cable.

Run mode

In Run mode the boxes move along the belt. The output switches each time a box passes the sensor, and the trace shows one pulse per box. The process data is 16 bits: distance in bits 4 to 15 in centimetres and OUT1 in bit 0. In the screenshot the master reads 02 31: 0x231 shifted right by four is 35 cm, and bit 0 is set.

That distance is the useful part. A PLC can tell a box near the rail from a box in the middle, or see that a box is lying across the belt.
