Tank level and overfill with one guided wave radar sensor

One sensor in the tank lid gives the PLC the level in millimetres and a hard “full” switch that does not depend on the PLC program.

11 October 2026 · Andrii Shylenko

The job

A metal tank holds a water-based medium, for example the rinse tank of a parts washer or a coolant reservoir. The PLC refills it and must never overfill it. Many plants use two float switches for this. A continuous level sensor replaces both and shows the level, so the operator sees the tank emptying long before the low alarm.

We use OUT1 as the overfill switch at 800 mm. The low alarm comes from the level value in the PLC.

The sensor

We used the ifm LR2050, a guided wave radar sensor. From the datasheet: probe length L 150 to 2000 mm, G 3/4 process connection, process temperature −20 to 100 °C, pressure rating 16 bar, measuring error ±7 mm, resolution 1 mm, sampling rate 4 Hz. Recommended media are water, water-based media, oils and oil-based media. Media with a dielectric constant of 1.8 to 5, such as oils, need a coaxial pipe.

Two limits depend on the probe you order. The set point SP is 15 to L−30 mm and the reset point rP is 10 to L−35 mm, in 1 mm steps, with a hysteresis of more than 5 mm. There is also an inactive range of 30 mm at the top and 10 mm at the bottom of the probe.

The setting, checked

We planned a 1000 mm probe. That makes SP at most 970 mm and rP at most 965 mm. SP 800 mm and rP 780 mm fit, and the 20 mm hysteresis is above the 5 mm minimum. The low alarm must sit above the bottom inactive range, so the PLC alarm at 150 mm is safe.

Be careful here. The studio does not know your probe length yet. It checks SP and rP against the longest probe, 2000 mm, so it shows 15–1970 mm. With a short probe you must check L−30 yourself. The studio does check that rP is below SP: set both to 800 and it answers “Reset point rP must be below Set point SP.”

The datasheet also has an EMC note: the emission limits are met in a closed metal tank. In a plastic or open tank a different EMC class applies. Check that before you mount it on a plastic tank.

Master, port and cable

Port X1 of a SICK SIG200, a 4-port IO-Link master with EtherNet/IP. The SICK datasheet gives up to 500 mA on pin 1 per port and 3 A for all outputs together. The LR2050 draws less than 50 mA and needs a class A port. The route in the model is 3.9 m, so the order has a 5 m M12 cable.

Run mode

In Run mode the tank fills and drains. The output switches on at 800 mm and stays on until the level falls to 780 mm. In the screenshot the level is 791 mm on the way down and OUT1 is still on. That is the hysteresis doing its job.

The process data is 16 bits: level in bits 2 to 15 at 1 mm per count, OUT2 in bit 1 and OUT1 in bit 0. The master reads 0C 5D: 0x0C5D shifted right by two is 791 mm, and bit 0 is set.

Validation is vendor 310, device 479. Cycle time 2.3 ms. Data storage is set to backup and restore, so a replacement sensor gets the switch points from the master. Write the probe length and medium on the sensor at commissioning; the studio does not export those two yet.

Buy and install

The order list has the SIG200, the LR2050 and a 5 m cable. The probe rod is a separate ifm accessory and is not in the studio's order list. Write the probe length you want in the install request. Buy and install sends the request to us, and we reply with a price and an install date. No payment is taken in the studio.
