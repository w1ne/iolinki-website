Integration and product status

Can I start without hardware?

Yes. Build the pinned v2.0.0 Linux host stack and run its tests. The host build and 22 CTest targets passed with GCC 13 on 1 October 2026. This is software evaluation; it does not establish physical IO-Link interoperability.

Is there a native IAR project that does not require CMake?

Yes: the STM32G0B1RE project includes source groups, C99 settings, ST IAR startup references and a linker file. Configure the CMSIS package paths as described in its README. The GCC firmware build is verified; native IAR compilation has not been run and needs an EWARM installation and license. See hardware and IDE status.

Which exact boards have build-verified examples?

Three reference projects have verified firmware builds: STM32G0B1RE with Arm GCC and TIOL112; Nucleo-U575ZI-Q with Zephyr 3.7.2 and TIOL112; and ESP32-C3-DevKitM-1 with ESP-IDF 5.4.0 and STEVAL-IOD003V1/L6362A. Source, build commands and wiring instructions are published. Native IAR compilation, complete analog LabWired Twins and physical-master validation remain pending. Embedded flash persistence is unsupported. None is presented as a validated turnkey physical device. Compare the exact projects and evidence.

Are TIOL112 and L6362A already integrated?

The published projects include TIOL112 and L6362A control drivers plus MCU UART, GPIO, wake-up and microsecond-timer adapters. Driver host tests and all three firmware builds pass. The ESP32-C3/L6362A example documents STEVAL-IOD003V1 headers, power settings and the required IN1 modification; it is an ESP-IDF project. These implementations are not yet proof of electrical interoperability or a physical-master exchange. Consult manufacturer documentation and validation status.

Which ESP32 example should I use instead of the experimental thermal sensor project?

Use the ESP32-C3 counter/button-and-LED reference. It provides an ESP-IDF application, L6362A adapter, build recipe and STEVAL-IOD003V1 wiring guide, with a verified firmware build. The older thermal-sensor repository is not the validated hardware reference for this path. The new example still needs physical wiring and commercial-master testing before it can be described as a demonstrated physical IO-Link device.

Has this been validated with a real commercial IO-Link master?

No published commercial-master physical interoperability report has been established. The separate iolinki-master stack and simulations are useful software checks. They do not substitute for bench results identifying a commercial master's model and firmware. See the report checklist.

Is there official IO-Link certification?

No published official certification record has been established. Protocol capabilities, passing software tests and certification are separate facts. Review current validation evidence.

Are there public deployed-customer references?

No public deployed-customer references have been established. We make no customer deployment claim. Use the published source, tests and validation status to scope your evaluation.

Commercial licensing

The answers below follow LICENSE.COMMERCIAL. Confirm the final scope and current terms in your quote and signed agreement.

What does the €1,399 Single Developer license cover?

The published one-time, royalty-free price covers one developer seat, full source access and unlimited commercial deployments. It includes one year of updates and bug fixes and email support with the response time stated in LICENSE.COMMERCIAL. Deployment count is not a developer-seat count.

What does the €4,699 Team license include?

The published one-time, royalty-free Team tier includes the Single Developer benefits, up to five developer seats, priority email support, quarterly technical reviews and up to eight hours of custom integration assistance. README wording has differed from the license document; these answers use LICENSE.COMMERCIAL. Confirm the assistance scope and applicable support terms in the quote.

Do the eight integration hours cover PHY porting and hardware integration?

The eight-hour allowance belongs to the Team tier; the Single Developer tier does not list it. The published terms describe custom integration assistance but do not define which hardware tasks are included. Agree the scope in the quote, including PHY callbacks, board wiring, toolchain, required equipment and validation work. The allowance alone does not promise a completed port, supplied hardware or certification. Until that scope is agreed, hardware-porting coverage is unresolved.

Do use rights expire after the first year?

The current terms retain perpetual rights to use the version you licensed. The included update period is one year. Extended support is optional and currently listed at 20% of the original license fee per year; confirm the renewal coverage in your agreement.

Can I ship compiled firmware or a system-on-module to my customers?

Binary redistribution as part of your product, including a system-on-module, is compatible with the prior clarification from the licensor by email. Confirm this use and its scope in the signed agreement. Redistributing iolinki source to third parties is a separate matter and needs explicitly agreed terms; buying a developer seat does not by itself define a source-resale license.

Is licensing tied to each DeviceID, downstream customer or processor target?

The published tiers are priced by developer seats and include unlimited commercial deployments. They do not list per-DeviceID, per-customer or per-processor fees. Confirm the exact distribution scope in your agreement, including compiled binaries supplied through a proprietary SOM SDK. The end-product manufacturer still defines its own VendorID, DeviceID, process data and IODD and validates the finished device; example identifiers are not a production identity allocation.

Can I evaluate under GPL without buying a commercial license?

The public code is available under GPLv3-or-later. Evaluation, internal use, modifying code and conveying software have different implications under the license. When you distribute a covered work, obligations can include corresponding source, license notices and the applicable GPL terms. “Evaluation” is not a blanket exemption, and making source visible alone does not resolve every obligation. Read the license for your use and distribution model. Commercial terms provide the proprietary licensing path.

Can I start evaluating my TIOL112 board before a commercial commitment?

Start with the public GPL source and the STM32G0B1RE/TIOL112 reference project; adapt the documented MCU callbacks and wiring to your board. This evaluation remains under the published GPL terms. No separate time-limited evaluation agreement is currently published. Contact the licensor if your process requires a defined evaluation period or different terms. Build verification does not replace testing your electronics with a physical master.

Is there an Enterprise option?

The published Enterprise tier is quoted individually and includes site-wide developer seats and negotiated services. Contact the licensor with your board, toolchain, product and integration needs. Custom scope and any SLA belong in that agreement.

Maintenance and evaluation help

What is the release cadence, and how are bugs prioritized?

No fixed release cadence or severity-based bug-priority policy is currently published. Review the actual release history, issue tracker and CI results. Include the affected commit, target, toolchain, impact and a reproducible case in a bug report. Commercial support response times follow the applicable license terms; a response time is not a guaranteed fix date. Any required release schedule, fix deadline or maintenance commitment needs explicit agreement.

How can I assess maintenance and report a problem?

Inspect releases, CI and issues. Include your release/commit, toolchain, target, reproduction commands and logs in a report. No additional response or release SLA is implied by these public resources; commercial support follows the published terms and your agreement.

What should I send when discussing an integration?

Include the MCU/board and PHY, IDE or RTOS, COM mode and process-data sizes, ISDU/data-storage needs, master model, schedule, developer-seat count and whether customers receive binaries or source. Start with the host evaluation and hardware matrix to identify the remaining work.
